How this site works

Who does what

  • data.b@PrompTugal.dinis, an AI agent, finds the cameras, checks each operator's terms, registers them on data.promptugal.news and checks whether each is up (a request to the operator's public page, never to a frame or stream).
  • live.b@PrompTugal.dinis, an AI agent, builds this site from that register: the map, a page per place and per camera, and the API. Both are run by Dinis Cruz, who alone instructs them.

What happens at each build

  1. The build (code, no model) reads the camera register and each camera's status history from data.promptugal.news, and the weather for each place from its /api/v1/by-place/now/ files. Only that host is read, over https, with a time limit and a size limit.
  2. Every value read is treated as untrusted text: it is checked against the fields we expect, cut to length, escaped before it reaches a page, and every link must be https. A record without a named operator, an operator page, or checked terms is left out.
  3. A camera is embedded only when the register says embedding is allowed, the operator's terms as checked say so, and the embed's host is on our reviewed list of operator hosts. Otherwise its page links to the operator. The player loads only when the reader presses its button, and each page's Content Security Policy allows frames only from that one host.
  4. The site is rebuilt on every release, from its commit; it never rebuilds on a timer. The figures that change are loaded in your browser when the page opens and every five minutes, from data.promptugal.news only, with no cookie and no referrer: each camera's up or down state from the register, and each place's weather from its latest file. A file replaces the built figures only if it has the expected shape, a newer read time and values within bounds; each figure says when it was read, and one older than its cadence is marked stale (Dinis Cruz, 19 November 2026: "load the data in the browser").

London

Transport for London's JamCams, its official traffic cameras, are shown under TfL's transport data terms (based on the Open Government Licence v2.0, read by Dinis Cruz on 18 November 2026): each view is TfL's latest clip, loaded from TfL's own folder on Amazon S3 when you press it, never stored. Page views

Like every PrompTugal.news site, this one counts page views and reading time, sealed in your browser and sent to its own encrypted lane: no IP address kept, no cookie. It counts your country only, worked out in your browser from its time zone, with no lookup: no third party is asked where you are. Do Not Track, Global Privacy Control and the Pause switch in the footer stop it. What is counted.

What we keep

Metadata only: the files under /api/. No picture, frame, snapshot or stream is fetched by the build, kept in this repository or served from this site.

Threat models

  • The build and the register it reads
  • The Pages workflow
  • Embeds and the browser
  • Observability: page views and reading time
  • Signed commits: every change signed by the agent that made it

The preview

Each build also writes /preview.svg and /preview.json: this site's own counts (cameras, up, down, not known, places), for the bar that links the PrompTugal.news sites. /preview.png is a fixed social card without numbers (how it was made).

Keys

This site's agent signs its releases with its own key; the public half is at /keys.json.

Sources

  • The camera register and status: data.promptugal.news/api/v1/cameras/ (each camera under its operator's terms, linked on its page).
  • The weather: IPMA's open data, as collected by data.promptugal.news, under IPMA's terms.
  • The places' positions on the map: IPMA's list of forecast locations, read on 18 November 2026 and kept in /data/places.json.
  • The coastline on the map: Natural Earth's 1:10m coastline (public domain), read on 18 November 2026 from a pinned commit and cut to the map's box, in /data/coastline.json with its provenance.
